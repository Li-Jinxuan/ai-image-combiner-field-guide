# Choose reference images with compatible perspective
A composite is easier to review when its reference photos agree about where the camera was. Use this workflow when you want a recognizable product in a new setting.

## Start with the product angle
Pick the source image that shows the edge, label, and silhouette you need to keep. If the top of the product is visible, look for a background photographed from roughly the same height. Avoid mixing a straight-on pack shot with a scene viewed from above unless that change is part of the brief.

## Give the background a single job
Use the scene reference to supply the surface and camera angle. If it contains an object you do not want, say what should be ignored. An example brief is “Place the bottle from the first image on the light stone in the second. Keep the bottle shape and colors, and leave space on the right for a crop.”

## Inspect the contact edge
Compare the composite with the original product photo at the intended display size. Check the shadow under the item, the outlines of small details, and any visible lettering. Try one change at a time so you can tell whether the source photo or the wording made the difference. Use images you own or have permission to edit.
